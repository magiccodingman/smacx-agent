## Important remaining gaps

- Deeper diplomacy negotiations still include several specialized counteroffer dialogs. Incoming energy/two-technology Governor-vote purchase offers are typed, confirmation-gated, and covered for rejection plus exact accepted payment. Reverse engineering proved that the apparent proposal-menu “trained military units” text is an unused `Script.txt` row: the executable never inserts it into `PROPOSAL`, while numeric ID 12 belongs to the separate Council-vote request path. It is therefore neither a legal missing action nor exposed as a capability. Direct native Pact-territory unit transfer and exact-amount energy gifts are implemented and deterministically covered. Reciprocal commlink sale/technology-exchange dialogs, third-party commlink/map offers, and outgoing multi-technology vote payments are implemented from exact script and engine context but still need deterministic native-effect regressions.
- Some uncommon specialized unit abilities and group commands still need semantic handlers and long-game encounter coverage. Alien Artifact base-entry choices are complete, including guarded consumption for technology or current Project/prototype minerals. Known owned bases are routable by object ID for every triad, visible friendly standalone airbases are routable by opaque tile ID, and owned carriers support capacity-reserved object-targeted recovery plus native boarding in single-player. Recovery routes and non-refueling sorties receive conservative execution-time fuel checks. Native Air Defense, bombing runs, general Former automation, combat On Alert, exploration, and land/sea native return are complete in single-player. Carrier recovery, bombing runs, terrain-improvement destruction, per-unit upgrades, and these persistent policies keep their LAN packet paths deliberately withheld until tested.
- Advanced governor production permissions are now fully observable and individually toggleable, including native production recalculation and the faction default inherited by future bases. Base obliteration is semantic in single-player; governor changes, obliteration, and several other base mutations still need LAN-specific synchronization and testing.
- Human LAN bargaining validates atomically committed Treaty, Pact, Blood Truce, exact technology transfer, bounded exact-energy transfer, exact contacted-third-faction joint-attack proposals, peer accept/decline, and end-transmission. A native network barrier preserves clause ordering inside each single outgoing command, and the recipient technology presentation uses a semantic LAN queue instead of entering SMACX's bridge-starving private `NetTechWindow` loop. The paired-human native record has no map-transfer clause; typed map exchanges belong to the separate AI negotiation dialogs. A focused two-process regression validates local multiplayer AI `COMM`/`COMMDIPLO` accept, `INTRONEW*` greeting continuation, passive `HELLO*`/`WHATSUP*`/rebuff notices, exact `DIPLO` finish, and safe rejection of fully described technology trades/demands or relationship offers while the peer stays nonmodal. Accepted AI transfers, concessions, and other consequential bargains remain fail-closed until their exact synchronized effects are covered. Several advanced unit/base command families also remain withheld in LAN as noted above. An older internal visual automation prototype exists but is not exposed by MCP and is not part of the no-vision agent contract.
- The production full-victory pipeline is deterministically covered without pixels or UI input: an actual completed game traverses credits, score report, Quayle rating, Hall of Fame, replay, final finish, and the native exit flags; a second economic-victory run also traverses its optional interlude prefix. Presentation-phase unit mutation and stale-action replay are rejected. Typed solo and LAN scenario launch are live-tested, including multiplayer faction selection and scenario-authored restrictions. A naturally reached long-play victory, optional external victory movies when `narrative_ui=true`, LAN packet semantics, and uncommon scenario-specific interactions still need further soak/implementation coverage.

An unknown modal is a hard stop, not permission to improvise UI input. The model must call `smac_report_capability_gap` and wait for bridge expansion. The bridge rejects its retired raw `act` operation even for authenticated direct clients, so this policy is enforced below the MCP layer.

## Custom match rules

Human-hosted games accept settings chosen through the human's native lobby
before managed clients join. DirectPlay synchronizes the complete native setup
record, and `smac_lan` decodes difficulty, map size, ocean/erosion/native-life/
cloud levels, timer, victory conditions, and ordinary game-rule toggles by
name. Raw masks remain available only as diagnostics.

AI-hosted games may use one of five guarded profiles or a fully typed custom
record. Custom input rejects unknown fields, out-of-range native IDs, the
timer's separate visual custom-clock editor, and the two random-leader options
that are not members of the synchronized LAN `GameRules` record. A real
two-process regression used intentionally mixed values and verified exact
convergence in each lobby and resulting game. Solo custom setup also supports
native custom dimensions and the random-leader toggles. Neither path opens a
setup menu or interprets a raw bit mask.

## Fair-play caveats

- Fogged and unknown tiles are never filtered using hidden occupants. A move into uncertainty may be queued and then resolved by the native engine, just as for a human player.
- Technology demands expose only the exact script-named bundle for the active native variant and revalidate player ownership before concession. Reciprocal counteroffers expose only a counterpart-owned technology the player does not own; no hidden blind-research target is read or returned.
- Agent-only and mixed DirectPlay are live-tested between independent native
  processes on one Linux host. Both AI-hosted and human-hosted paths cover
  exact identity, chat, checkpoint, disconnect/rejoin, saved factions, and
  continuation. Physical two-machine macvlan/ipvlan compatibility and firewall
  behavior still require certification.
